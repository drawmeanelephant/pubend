# Alaska Yellow-cedar

## Field marks

Alaska yellow-cedar, Callitropsis nootkatensis, is an elegant tree with drooping branchlets and loose, somewhat flattened sprays of scale leaves. The foliage is usually darker and less glossy than western redcedar, and the sprays tend to hang rather than spread in crisp horizontal fans. Cones are small, roundish, and woody or leathery, often requiring two seasons to mature. Bark is thin, gray-brown, and shreddy or plated on older trunks. Cut wood has a distinctive yellow color and a fine, even texture, which gives the tree its common name.

## Habitat

The species is most common in cool, wet coastal mountains and northern forests, but it also appears in high-elevation Cascadian sites. It tolerates poor, saturated soils and deep snow better than many competitors, allowing it to persist on cold slopes, muskeg margins, and exposed ridges. Alaska yellow-cedar is slow-growing and can be very old relative to its size. In recent decades widespread crown dieback and mortality have been observed in parts of its range, particularly where reduced snow insulation exposes shallow roots to freezing injury.

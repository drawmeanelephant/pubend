# Western Redcedar

## Field marks

Western redcedar, Thuja plicata, carries glossy green scale leaves in flattened sprays that often droop slightly at their tips. The cones are small, narrow, and woody, opening into a shape sometimes compared to a tiny flower or rosette. Bark on mature trunks is reddish brown to gray-brown, fibrous, and easy to peel in vertical strips. Large trees frequently develop buttressed bases, hollow cores, or heavily branching crowns, especially when growing in wet forests. The foliage is aromatic, with a sweet resinous scent when crushed.

## Habitat and significance

The species is characteristic of moist coastal and lower montane forests, but it also occurs on wet interior sites where seepage or deep snow maintains soil moisture. It grows with western hemlock, Sitka spruce, Douglas-fir, grand fir, and shade-tolerant understory plants. Western redcedar wood is light, durable, and resistant to decay, and the species has central importance in the material cultures of many Indigenous peoples of the Northwest Coast. Bark, roots, withes, and wood have long been used for weaving, carving, construction, and canoe building.

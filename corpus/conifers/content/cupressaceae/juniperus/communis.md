# Common Juniper

## Field marks

Common juniper, Juniperus communis, bears sharp awl-shaped needles in whorls of three. The needles have a pale stomatal band above and are stiff enough to make dense foliage distinctly prickly. Plants vary from low spreading mats to upright shrubs depending on exposure, browsing, elevation, and local genetic variation. The reproductive structures are small fleshy cones that ripen to a blue-black color with a whitish bloom. Despite their berry-like appearance and culinary use in some regions, they are modified cones rather than true berries.

## Habitat

The species has a broad northern distribution and occurs in open forests, rocky slopes, subalpine meadows, and cold dry sites. In the Pacific Northwest it is less visually dominant than the large forest conifers, but it can be important in exposed montane habitats. Low forms stabilize thin soils, provide cover, and persist where wind and snow prune upright growth. The [mountain form](communis/montana.md#field-marks) is treated separately because its spreading habit and habitat can make it look unlike the shrubbier plants encountered at lower elevations.

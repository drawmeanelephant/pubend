# Mountain Common Juniper

## Field marks

Mountain common juniper is treated here as Juniperus communis var. montana, although botanical treatments vary in how they separate and name low-growing forms of common juniper. It typically forms a dense, spreading mat or low shrub rather than an upright bush. Needles retain the species-level arrangement in whorls of three, with sharp tips and pale upper stomatal bands. Branchlets are often close to the ground or tightly layered, an architecture that resists wind damage and sheds or distributes snow loads differently than a taller shrub would.

## Habitat

This form is most likely on exposed montane slopes, rocky ridges, subalpine meadow margins, and other cold sites with thin soil. It may grow beneath scattered conifers or form low patches in open terrain. Because growth habit is strongly shaped by environment, identification should not rely on stature alone; needle whorls, cone structure, and continuity with nearby common juniper populations are equally important. The [common juniper species account](../communis.md) gives the species-level characters, while this variety account is best used as a practical field distinction rather than as a rigid taxonomic boundary.

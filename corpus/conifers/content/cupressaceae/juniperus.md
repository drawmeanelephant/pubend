# Juniperus

Juniperus, the junipers, is a widespread genus whose species may be trees, shrubs, or low mats depending on climate and lineage. Many have awl-shaped juvenile leaves, scale-like adult leaves, or both on the same plant. Their most familiar reproductive structures are fleshy cones that resemble berries, though they are botanically cones with fused scales rather than true fruits. Junipers frequently occupy exposed, dry, rocky, or cold environments where broad-crowned trees grow poorly, and their forms often record wind, snow, and browsing pressure.

The guide includes [common juniper](juniperus/communis.md), a circumboreal species with sharp awl-shaped leaves and a wide range of growth habits. Because the species varies strongly with exposure, the nested variety account is useful when interpreting the low montane forms encountered in parts of the Pacific Northwest.

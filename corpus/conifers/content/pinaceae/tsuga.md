# Tsuga

Tsuga, the hemlocks, have short, slender needles with rounded or only slightly pointed tips. The needles attach by delicate stalks and often appear unequal in length, producing a soft, lacy foliage texture. Their cones are small, pendulous, and woody, usually no longer than a thumb nail or small finger segment depending on the species. A drooping terminal leader is a classic hemlock habit, particularly visible in young trees, though age, damage, and exposure can obscure it.

The genus is represented here by two species that divide the landscape by elevation and snow regime. [Western hemlock](tsuga/heterophylla.md) is a defining tree of moist lowland and montane forests west of the Cascade crest. [Mountain hemlock](tsuga/mertensiana.md) occupies colder subalpine terrain, where heavy snow shapes its narrower crown and more compact branching. Needle arrangement provides a dependable supplement to habitat when separating them.

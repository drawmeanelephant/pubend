# Pseudotsuga

Pseudotsuga is a small genus whose best-known member is not a true fir despite its common name. Its needles are single, flat, and soft, and its leaf scars are rounded rather than covered by the persistent pegs found in spruces. The most decisive field characters are often reproductive: mature cones hang downward and display thin, three-pointed bracts that project beyond the cone scales. Sharp-pointed winter buds are another useful clue, especially when cones are absent or high above the ground.

In the Pacific Northwest the genus is dominated by [Pseudotsuga menziesii](pseudotsuga/menziesii.md), Douglas-fir, a species of immense ecological and economic importance. Its broad climatic amplitude means that the species account must be read together with the two regional variety accounts nested beneath it, because coastal and interior trees differ in stature, foliage color, cold tolerance, and habitat preference.

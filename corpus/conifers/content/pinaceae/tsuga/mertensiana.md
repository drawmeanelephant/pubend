# Mountain Hemlock

## Field marks

Mountain hemlock, Tsuga mertensiana, is usually a more compact and bluish-green tree than western hemlock. Its needles are less strongly flattened into two-ranked sprays and instead radiate more evenly around the twig, giving foliage a fuller or slightly cylindrical look. Needles are short and may carry a blue cast from surface wax. Cones are larger than those of western hemlock but still small compared with many other conifers, hanging at branch tips and maturing to purplish or brown tones. In exposed subalpine sites the crown can become narrow, irregular, or flagged by wind.

## Habitat

The species occupies cold, snowy montane and subalpine forests, especially near timberline in the Cascades and coastal ranges. It often grows with Pacific silver fir, subalpine fir, Alaska yellow-cedar, and whitebark pine. Heavy snow loading strongly shapes its form, favoring flexible branches and narrow crowns. Mountain hemlock grows slowly in severe sites but can be long-lived, and even modest-looking individuals may be much older than their size suggests. It is poorly adapted to hot, dry lowland conditions.

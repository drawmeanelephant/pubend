# Western Hemlock

## Field marks

Western hemlock, Tsuga heterophylla, has short needles of distinctly unequal length arranged in flat, delicate sprays. Needle undersides bear pale stomatal lines, and the foliage has a fine, lacy texture compared with the stiffer needles of spruce or fir. Cones are small, brown, and pendulous, often abundant near the ends of branches. The terminal shoot commonly droops, giving even young trees a graceful profile, while mature forest trees may develop massive trunks and broad crowns. Bark becomes thick, dark reddish brown, and deeply furrowed with age.

## Habitat

Western hemlock is a hallmark species of moist forests west of the Cascade crest. It is highly shade tolerant and can germinate on rotten logs, stumps, and humus-rich forest floors where small-seeded competitors struggle. Over long periods without major disturbance it often replaces Douglas-fir in the canopy because its seedlings persist in shade and respond quickly when gaps open. Western hemlock grows with western redcedar, Sitka spruce, Pacific silver fir, Douglas-fir, and a dense understory of shade-loving shrubs and ferns.

# Pacific Silver Fir

## Field marks

Pacific silver fir, Abies amabilis, is a montane true fir with dense, dark foliage and a narrow, pointed crown. Its needles are shorter and more crowded than those of grand fir, often curving upward on sunlit shoots but retaining a flattened appearance on shaded branches. The undersides carry conspicuous silvery-white stomatal bands, a feature reflected in the common name. Bark is smooth and pale gray with resin blisters on young stems, becoming plated or shallowly furrowed on old trunks. The upright cones disintegrate in place, as they do in other Abies species.

## Habitat

This species favors cool, wet mountain slopes and is especially characteristic of mid- to upper-elevation forests in the Cascades and coastal ranges. It often grows with mountain hemlock, western hemlock, Alaska yellow-cedar, and subalpine fir where snowpack is deep and summers are cool. Pacific silver fir tolerates shade when young and can form dense regeneration beneath canopy gaps. Near its upper elevational limits it becomes shorter and more irregular, with snow loading and wind pruning shaping the crown.

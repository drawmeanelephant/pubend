# Pseudotsuga menziesii

Pseudotsuga menziesii, Douglas-fir, is one of the most consequential trees in western North America. It combines fast growth, great potential height, thick fire-resistant bark in mature individuals, and wood valued for strength and dimensional stability. The needles are flat, soft, and fragrant when crushed; the buds are narrow and sharply pointed; and the hanging cones have distinctive three-pointed bracts that extend beyond each scale. Young forest-grown trees often retain a narrow pointed crown, while old open-grown trees can become massive, deeply furrowed, and irregular.

The species spans a broad climatic gradient, and botanists commonly recognize regional varieties rather than treating all populations as uniform. The [coastal Douglas-fir](menziesii/coastal.md#field-marks) account describes the tall maritime form west of the Cascade crest. The [Rocky Mountain Douglas-fir](menziesii/rocky-mountain.md#field-marks) account covers the smaller, colder-adapted interior form. These varieties intergrade in places, but separating them is still useful when interpreting growth habit, foliage color, and forest associations.

# Coastal Douglas-fir

## Field marks

Coastal Douglas-fir, commonly treated as Pseudotsuga menziesii var. menziesii, is the maritime form of the species. In favorable lowland and lower montane sites it can become exceptionally tall, with a straight trunk, deeply furrowed mature bark, and foliage that is usually richer green than that of interior trees. Needles are soft, single, and arranged around the twig but often twist into a flattened spray. The pointed buds and hanging cones with three-pointed bracts are consistent species-level characters, while the stature and habitat often supply the first hint that the coastal variety is involved.

## Habitat

This variety dominates or co-dominates many forests west of the Cascade crest, especially on well-drained slopes and benches. It associates with western hemlock, western redcedar, grand fir, bigleaf maple, and red alder depending on moisture, disturbance history, and elevation. Thick bark allows older trees to survive low- to moderate-severity fire, while seedlings generally establish best in mineral soil or open canopy conditions. In very wet coastal forests, shade-tolerant hemlock and cedar may eventually replace it where large disturbances are rare. For cone bracts, buds, and other species-level characters shared by both varieties, return to the [Douglas-fir species account](../menziesii.md).

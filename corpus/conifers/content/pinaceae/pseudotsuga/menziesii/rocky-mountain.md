# Rocky Mountain Douglas-fir

## Field marks

Rocky Mountain Douglas-fir, usually called Pseudotsuga menziesii var. glauca, is the interior form of the species. Compared with coastal trees it is generally smaller, slower growing, and better adapted to cold winters, periodic drought, and shorter growing seasons. The foliage often has a blue-green or gray-green cast, though color alone is not a dependable identification character. Buds, needle structure, and the characteristic three-pointed cone bracts remain the same at the species level, so geography and habitat are often the most practical first clues.

## Habitat

This variety occurs in the interior Pacific Northwest, especially east of the Cascade crest and in the mountains of Idaho, eastern British Columbia, and neighboring regions. It is common on dry slopes, in mixed-conifer forests, and in fire-shaped landscapes where ponderosa pine, western larch, or lodgepole pine may share the canopy. Its cold tolerance allows it to occupy sites too severe for coastal populations, but its growth form is highly responsive to exposure, snow, and recurring fire. Where interior and coastal populations intergrade, the [Douglas-fir species account](../menziesii.md) is more dependable than foliage color alone.

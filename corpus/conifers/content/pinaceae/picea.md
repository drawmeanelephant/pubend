# Picea

Picea, the spruces, is defined in the field by sharply pointed needles attached singly to tiny woody pegs called sterigmata. When a needle falls, the peg remains, giving spruce twigs a rough texture that can be felt even after the foliage has been shed. Needles are usually four-sided or only slightly flattened, so they roll between the fingers rather than lying flat. Spruce cones hang downward, have thin flexible scales, and usually fall intact instead of disintegrating on the branch as fir cones do.

This guide treats two species with contrasting distributions. [Sitka spruce](picea/sitchensis.md) is a giant of the maritime fog belt, while [Engelmann spruce](picea/engelmannii.md) is centered in colder interior and high-elevation forests. Their separation is often easiest by geography, but needle form, cone scale shape, and crown character provide additional evidence where the species approach one another.

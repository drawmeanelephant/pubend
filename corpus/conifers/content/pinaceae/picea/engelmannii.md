# Engelmann Spruce

## Field marks

Engelmann spruce, Picea engelmannii, is a cold-adapted spruce of interior mountains and high elevations. Its needles are four-sided enough to roll between the fingers and are usually less harshly sharp than those of Sitka spruce, though they remain prickly. The hanging cones are small to medium-sized, slender, and covered with thin papery scales. Crowns are typically narrow and spire-like, an efficient shape for shedding heavy snow, and old trees in exposed sites may become flagged or irregular on the windward side.

## Habitat

The species is most at home in cool upper montane and subalpine forests, where it often grows with subalpine fir, mountain hemlock, lodgepole pine, or whitebark pine depending on latitude and local climate. It can occupy valley bottoms in cold continental interiors and then reappear at much higher elevations farther west. Engelmann spruce tolerates long winters and deep snow but grows slowly under severe exposure. At treeline it may persist as scattered upright trees rather than forming the low krummholz mats typical of some other subalpine conifers.

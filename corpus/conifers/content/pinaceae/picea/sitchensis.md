# Sitka Spruce

## Field marks

Sitka spruce, Picea sitchensis, is a coastal giant with sharp, stiff needles that are somewhat flattened and difficult to roll between the fingers. Like other spruces, its needles grow from persistent pegs, so a bare twig feels rough and raspy. Mature cones are slender, hanging, and papery, with thin scales that are not woody and rigid like pine cone scales. The bark is thin and scaly on young trees, later breaking into gray-brown plates. In open coastal sites the crown can be broad and heavily branched, while forest-grown trees develop long clean trunks.

## Habitat

The species is concentrated in the maritime fog belt, often within a relatively narrow distance of the Pacific coast. It favors moist, cool, nutrient-rich sites and can dominate river terraces, coastal benches, and lower mountain slopes. Sitka spruce commonly grows with western hemlock, western redcedar, red alder, and salmonberry in the northern rainforest. Salt-laden wind, abundant moisture, and mild temperatures suit it well, but it becomes much less competitive away from maritime influence.

# Taxaceae

Taxaceae, the yew family, is represented in the Pacific Northwest by a small number of shade-tolerant trees and shrubs. Yews differ sharply from the cone-bearing image many people associate with conifers: their seeds are not enclosed in a woody cone but sit partly exposed within a fleshy, cup-shaped aril. The leaves are flat, narrow, and usually arranged in two ranks along the twig. The family is also notable for the presence of toxic alkaloids in much of the plant tissue, so attractive red arils should never be treated as edible fruit.

Only one genus is needed in this compact guide. [Taxus](taxaceae/taxus.md) contains Pacific yew, a slow-growing understory tree whose ecological importance is disproportionate to its modest size. Its sparse branching, thin reddish-brown bark, and dark green needles make it distinctive once the family-level clues are understood.

# Cupressaceae

Cupressaceae, the cypress or cedar family, is diverse in leaf form but easier to approach through a few repeated habits. Most species have scale-like or awl-shaped leaves pressed closely to the twig or arranged in small sprays, and many bear small cones that are woody, leathery, or berry-like. In the Pacific Northwest this family is especially associated with wet coastal forests, cool montane slopes, and exposed rocky sites. Several of its species are long-lived and develop highly weather-resistant wood, a trait with deep ecological and cultural importance.

The included genera illustrate three different expressions of the family. [Thuja](cupressaceae/thuja.md) contains western redcedar, with flattened sprays of scale leaves and narrow woody cones. [Callitropsis](cupressaceae/callitropsis.md) contains Alaska yellow-cedar, a drooping montane tree whose generic placement has shifted among several names. [Juniperus](cupressaceae/juniperus.md) covers junipers, including a widespread species whose fleshy cones look superficially like berries.

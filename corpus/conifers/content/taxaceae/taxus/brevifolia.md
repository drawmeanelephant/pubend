# Pacific Yew

## Field marks

Pacific yew, Taxus brevifolia, is a small to medium-sized tree or large shrub with dark green needles arranged in flat sprays. Individual needles are short, narrow, and pointed but not stiffly sharp like spruce needles. The bark is thin and reddish brown to purplish brown, often peeling in small flakes and revealing reddish tones beneath. Seeds are borne singly within a bright red, cup-shaped fleshy aril rather than inside a woody cone. The trunk is frequently irregular, fluted, or multi-stemmed, reflecting slow growth in shaded understory conditions.

## Habitat and cautions

The species grows in moist coniferous forests, canyon bottoms, shaded slopes, and streamside sites from coastal lowlands into montane terrain. It often persists beneath taller Douglas-fir, hemlock, or cedar canopies and may remain small for many decades. Pacific yew wood is dense, strong, and flexible, and the species became widely known because bark tissue supplied precursors for the cancer drug paclitaxel. Its foliage and seeds are toxic if ingested, and identification should never involve tasting the red aril or any other part of the plant.

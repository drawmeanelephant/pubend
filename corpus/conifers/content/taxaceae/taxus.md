# Taxus

Taxus, the yews, are evergreen trees and shrubs with flat linear needles and seeds partly surrounded by a fleshy aril. Unlike most conifers in this guide, yews do not produce a woody cone. Their needles commonly twist at the base so that they appear two-ranked along the branchlets, creating flat, dark green sprays. Bark is thin, often reddish or purplish brown, and tends to flake or peel in small scales. Most plant tissues contain toxic compounds, including the seed itself, so yews should be treated as poisonous despite the soft appearance of the aril.

The Pacific Northwest representative is [Pacific yew](taxus/brevifolia.md), an understory species whose slow growth and irregular form contrast sharply with the tall straight conifers around it. Its species account focuses on foliage arrangement, bark, reproductive structures, and the shaded habitats where it persists.

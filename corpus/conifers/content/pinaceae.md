# Pinaceae

Pinaceae, the pine family, is the largest conifer family represented in this guide. Its members bear needle-like leaves, produce woody cones, and often carry resin in their bark, wood, or foliage. In the Pacific Northwest the family supplies many of the region's tallest and most commercially important trees, but the family account is most useful as an identification threshold: look first at how needles attach to the twig, then at the form and position of mature cones. Those characters are more dependable than height or general silhouette, which vary greatly with age and exposure.

This guide follows four genera within the family. [Pseudotsuga](pinaceae/pseudotsuga.md) is represented by Douglas-fir, a tree with singular buds and conspicuous three-pointed cone bracts. [Picea](pinaceae/picea.md) covers the spruces, whose sharp needles grow from small persistent pegs. [Abies](pinaceae/abies.md) includes the true firs, with flat needles and upright cones that disintegrate on the branch. [Tsuga](pinaceae/tsuga.md) treats the hemlocks, recognized by short needles, small cones, and often gracefully drooping leaders.

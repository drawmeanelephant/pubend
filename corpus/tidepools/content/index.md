# Tidepools as a Living Lattice

A tidepool is never just a puddle left behind by the sea. It is a small basin where lunar timing, rock shape, wave force, hunger, shelter, and patience meet in plain sight. This guide treats the rocky intertidal as a lattice rather than a ladder: the same animal may be understood through physiology, weather, habitat geometry, and a single memorable morning in the field. Begin with [the mixed semidiurnal rhythm](orientation/tide-cycle.md#reading-a-mixed-semidiurnal-day), learn [the first scan](fieldcraft/observation.md#the-first-scan) before touching anything, and keep the shape of [the food web](life/intertidal-foodweb.md#who-eats-whom) in mind while you watch.

The site is organized so a reader can enter almost anywhere. A habitat page may lead to a fish, a fish may lead back to kelp, and a journal entry may explain why a theoretically good low tide was useless in practice. For orientation, [kelp edges](habitats/kelp-edges.md#where-forest-meets-rock) show how a forest offshore changes life on bare rock, while [the spring equinox journal](journal/spring-equinox.md#when-the-crowd-arrives) shows what happens when timing, sunlight, and crowds converge. The hierarchy gives the collection shelves; the links are the tide moving through them.

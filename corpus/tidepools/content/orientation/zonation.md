# Zonation and Horizontal Bands

## Horizontal bands

Zonation is the visible sorting of organisms into bands across the intertidal. The pattern can look simple from a distance: dark algae high on the rock, mussel beds below, then richer pools and kelp toward the low shore. Up close, every band is a compromise. Physical stress limits many organisms toward the top, while competition and predation often press upward from below. The mechanics of [hanging on](../life/adaptations.md#hanging-on) make more sense when you see how little time a high-shore animal has to feed compared with a low-shore neighbor.

Rock shape can sharpen these bands or scramble them. A stepped surface such as [a rocky bench](../habitats/rocky-benches.md#benches-as-stair-steps) traps water at different elevations and lets bands widen into patches. Small mobile animals complicate the picture further: [black tegula snails](../species/black-tegula.md#heat-and-desiccation) cluster in crevices during heat, then range farther when the rock is cool and wet.

## Bands as forecasts

The best use of zonation is predictive but humble. If you know the tidal height reached by a particular band, you can estimate when it will emerge or disappear by reading [the tide cycle](tide-cycle.md#reading-a-mixed-semidiurnal-day). After large surf, bands may look smeared or reset because animals have been buried, torn away, or redistributed; [the storm journal](../journal/storm-aftermath.md#what-the-swell-rearranged) describes one such rearrangement. The bands are therefore neither permanent nor imaginary. They are a statistical memory written in living tissue.

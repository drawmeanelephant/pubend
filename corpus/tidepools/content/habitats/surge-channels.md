# Surge Channels

## Reading whitewater

Surge channels are narrow cuts where incoming water accelerates, turns, and sometimes rebounds. They can look inviting because their walls are shaded and crowded with life, yet the same geometry concentrates force. Before entering or even leaning over one, study the whitewater rhythm and compare it with the cautionary habits in [reading the ocean](../orientation/safety-and-ethics.md#reading-the-ocean). Foam lines often reveal whether water is merely filling the channel or driving through it with enough power to lift gravel.

The animals inside these channels are not necessarily more delicate than those outside. Many are simply positioned to avoid the main vector of flow. The broad lesson of [hanging on](../life/adaptations.md#hanging-on) becomes three-dimensional here: ceiling, wall, and floor each offer different exposure.

## The crawlspace forest

At low tide a channel can feel like a crawlspace beneath the shore. Kelp fragments lodge along the walls, crabs move through shadow, and sponges or tunicates occupy surfaces that are rarely dry. A [kelp crab](../species/kelp-crab.md#not-quite-a-decorator) may be obvious only when it crosses a patch of light. Because the visual field is cramped, equipment discipline matters; the small kit described in [what to carry](../fieldcraft/equipment.md#what-to-carry) is safer than a pack that shifts balance. Night intensifies the effect, as described in [lights and shadows](../journal/night-low-tide.md#lights-and-shadows).

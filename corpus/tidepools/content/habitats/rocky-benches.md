# Rocky Benches

## Benches as stair steps

Rocky benches are broad platforms cut or fractured into shallow steps. Each step holds water differently: upper surfaces dry quickly, lower lips stay slick, and shallow pans extend the feeding time of animals that would otherwise be exposed. Because the steps compress several tidal heights into a small horizontal distance, benches are ideal places to compare theory with observation. Stand back and look for [horizontal bands](../orientation/zonation.md#horizontal-bands), then crouch to see how cracks and tiny rims soften those bands into islands.

The physical rhythm of emergence follows the tide, but not mechanically. A surge may wash a high step long after it appears dry, while a sheltered depression may stay isolated. Learning [when the rise begins](../orientation/tide-cycle.md#watch-the-rise) is therefore both a safety practice and an interpretive tool.

## Exposure clock

Every exposed organism on a bench is working against a clock. A sea star can continue moving only while enough water remains for its tube feet; a snail can graze only while its foot and mantle stay wet. Watching [black tegula](../species/black-tegula.md#heat-and-desiccation) cluster in a seam or [an ochre sea star](../species/ochre-starfish.md#keystone-predation) wedge into a mussel bed makes the clock visible in behavior. After storms, that clock may be reset by scouring or burial, as recorded in [the storm-aftermath notes](../journal/storm-aftermath.md#what-the-swell-rearranged).

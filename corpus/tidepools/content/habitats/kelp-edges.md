# Kelp Edges

## Where forest meets rock

Kelp edges are places where the offshore forest touches the intertidal without fully entering it. Fronds bend into the lowest pools during high water, fragments lodge among rocks after surf, and animals that spend much of their lives in subtidal water become temporary visitors. This edge is a subsidy: food, shade, and shelter arrive from a larger system. It is a key extension of [the intertidal food web](../life/intertidal-foodweb.md#who-eats-whom), because energy here often began well outside the visible shore.

The edge also carries conflict. [Purple urchins](../species/purple-urchin.md#grazing-pressure) can consume young kelp and drift algae, and when their grazing outweighs kelp recruitment the border retreats. Fish such as [opaleye](../species/opaleye.md#algae-browser) move along the margin with more flexibility, nipping algae and small animals wherever the water is deep enough.

## Movement at the margin

Because the edge is mobile, it rewards repeated observation. A calm morning may show only a few trapped blades, while the same place after wind can be littered with torn fronds. [Kelp crabs](../species/kelp-crab.md#not-quite-a-decorator) exploit both living and detached material, shifting their camouflage as the habitat shifts. Tide height determines access; check [slack water](../fieldcraft/tide-tables.md#slack-water), but remember that floating kelp can make even shallow pools difficult to read.

# Sand and Gravel Pockets

## The mobile floor

Sand and gravel pockets are deceptive because they look empty compared with encrusted rock. In fact, the particles themselves are habitat: spaces between grains hold water, organic debris collects in seams, and small animals use shallow burial as protection from both sun and surf. The cost is instability. Every strong wave can rearrange the floor, burying some organisms and exposing others. Adaptations described under [water loss](../life/adaptations.md#water-loss) take a different form here because the substrate can simultaneously shield and abrade.

Careful walking matters. A footstep that seems harmless on loose gravel can crush animals beneath the surface or collapse the drainage structure of a small pocket. The principle of [leave no trace](../orientation/safety-and-ethics.md#leave-no-trace) therefore applies even where there is no obvious attached life.

## Nursery pools

Shallow sandy pools sometimes act as nurseries for young fish and mobile crustaceans, offering warmth and food at the cost of sudden isolation. Their occupants can be difficult to interpret because a single tide may transform refuge into trap. After heavy surf, the grain size and depth of these pools may change overnight; [the storm journal](../journal/storm-aftermath.md#what-the-swell-rearranged) records how quickly the floor can be rewritten. Compare these patches with idealized [zonation](../orientation/zonation.md#horizontal-bands), but expect mobility to blur the lines. During spring lows, [nursery pools](../journal/spring-equinox.md#nursery-pools) may hold juvenile fish in water only a few centimeters deep.

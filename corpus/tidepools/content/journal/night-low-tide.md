# Night Low Tide

## Lights and shadows

A night low tide compresses the shore into whatever a beam of light can hold. The pool that seemed simple in daylight becomes layered: bright surface reflections, moving shadows beneath algae, and animals whose colors appear only at close range. A narrow flashlight is more useful than a broad lantern because it preserves contrast, but it also means that the route must be planned with care. I followed the equipment habits in [what to carry](../fieldcraft/equipment.md#what-to-carry), keeping one hand free and the beam low rather than sweeping it across the water.

The slower scan described in [observation practice](../fieldcraft/observation.md#the-first-scan) becomes even more important at night because peripheral vision is unreliable. Sounds carry farther, too: gravel clicks, water threads through hidden passages, and small animals drop into pools before they are seen.

## Animals after dark

Some animals seem bolder when the shore is dark. A [kelp crab](../species/kelp-crab.md#not-quite-a-decorator) crossed open rock between two pieces of algae, something I had rarely watched it do by day. In the channel, shadows made the structural complexity of [the crawlspace forest](../habitats/surge-channels.md#the-crawlspace-forest) more obvious, though not safer. The tide did not pause for the darkness; [watching the rise](../orientation/tide-cycle.md#watch-the-rise) was still the discipline that ended the visit.

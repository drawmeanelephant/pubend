# Spring Equinox Low Tide

## When the crowd arrives

The equinox low was predicted well in advance, and by sunrise the access trail already carried a loose procession of families, students, photographers, and solitary regulars. The tide table had promised a generous window, but only the first hour felt spacious. I had checked [slack water](../fieldcraft/tide-tables.md#slack-water) the night before and still arrived later than intended, which meant that the broad outer bench was already dotted with people moving in the careful sideways walk of wet rock.

Crowds change what can be observed. Skittish fish retreat, birds keep their distance, and the temptation to compete for spectacular finds replaces the slower work of comparison. The bands described in [horizontal zonation](../orientation/zonation.md#horizontal-bands) were nevertheless clear because wetness boundaries remained visible even where animals had retreated.

## Nursery pools

The best observation came from a shallow sandy pocket where juvenile fish flickered over rippled grains. It was a reminder that apparently poor habitat can become important for a very specific hour, especially in [sand and gravel pockets](../habitats/sand-and-gravel.md#the-mobile-floor). Nearby, a contracted [giant green anemone](../species/giant-green-anemone.md#green-in-the-flesh) showed how little of a tidepool animal is visible when it chooses defense over display. I left before the turning tide, using [the first scan](../fieldcraft/observation.md#the-first-scan) one last time from the upper shelf and noticing how much more the pool revealed when no one was leaning over it.

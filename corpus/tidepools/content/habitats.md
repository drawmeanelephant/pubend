# Habitat Mosaic

A shore is a mosaic, not a single habitat repeated at different heights. Rock benches, surge channels, kelp margins, and mobile sand each filter the same tides into different physical regimes. Organisms respond to those regimes, but they also alter them: mussels create shelter, surfgrass traps sediment, urchins clear algae, and burrowing animals loosen the floor. This section begins with [rocky benches](habitats/rocky-benches.md#benches-as-stair-steps), moves through [surge channels](habitats/surge-channels.md#the-crawlspace-forest) and [kelp edges](habitats/kelp-edges.md#where-forest-meets-rock), and ends on [the mobile floor](habitats/sand-and-gravel.md#the-mobile-floor).

The mosaic matters because a species seen in one setting may be playing a different role in another. A crab sheltering under kelp is not necessarily the same ecological actor as that crab in a narrow channel. Likewise, the stress of exposure on open rock makes [the adaptations of attachment](life/adaptations.md#hanging-on) much easier to understand than they would be in a calm pool. Use habitat pages as lenses, not boxes.

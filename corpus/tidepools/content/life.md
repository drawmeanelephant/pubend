# Life Strategies in the Intertidal

Intertidal organisms live in a place that alternates between ocean and weather. They must handle immersion and air, feeding and fasting, heat and cold, sometimes within the same hour. The result is not a simple list of tough species but a set of strategies that trade one risk against another. Some animals clamp down and wait, some retreat into crevices, some use neighbors as living shelter, and some influence the whole community. The central ideas are [keystone interactions](life/keystone-species.md#removal-experiments), the mechanics of [hanging on](life/adaptations.md#hanging-on), and the tangled question of [who eats whom](life/intertidal-foodweb.md#who-eats-whom).

Species accounts in this guide are deliberately connected to those ideas rather than isolated as collectible facts. The green color of [the giant green anemone](species/giant-green-anemone.md#green-in-the-flesh) is a story about symbiosis and light, but also about shallow pools and cloudy water. Even habitat structure changes the meaning of adaptation: in [surge channels](habitats/surge-channels.md#the-crawlspace-forest), the same body shape that prevents drying may also reduce drag when the sea suddenly accelerates.

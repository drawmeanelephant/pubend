# Tidepool Field Journal

These journal entries are not weather reports or species checklists. They are records of attention: what was visible, what was missed, and what changed when the observer returned with a slightly better question. Because the intertidal is episodic, a single visit often teaches less than the comparison between visits. The entries cover [the spring equinox crowd](journal/spring-equinox.md#when-the-crowd-arrives), [storm-rearranged rock](journal/storm-aftermath.md#what-the-swell-rearranged), and [the compressed world of a night low tide](journal/night-low-tide.md#lights-and-shadows).

The notes frequently contradict the cleaner explanations elsewhere in the guide, and that is intentional. Tide tables predict water height but not visibility; diagrams of zonation predict bands but not the exact spray line after a week of wind. Cross-check a planned outing against [slack water](fieldcraft/tide-tables.md#slack-water), then compare what you find with [the horizontal bands](orientation/zonation.md#horizontal-bands) without expecting either source to be sufficient alone.

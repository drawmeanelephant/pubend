# Opaleye

## Algae browser

Opaleye, *Girella nigricans*, are deep-bodied fish often seen flashing sideways through shallow water near rock and kelp. They eat algae, but calling them simple herbivores understates their flexibility; small invertebrates and drifting material also enter the diet. That mixed feeding makes them useful connective tissue in [the food web](../life/intertidal-foodweb.md#who-eats-whom), especially along [kelp edges](../habitats/kelp-edges.md#where-forest-meets-rock) where plant material, animal prey, and shelter all shift with the tide.

The species can look abundant one minute and absent the next because small groups move between deeper channels and temporary pools. At night their behavior becomes harder to interpret; the compressed visibility described in [lights and shadows](../journal/night-low-tide.md#lights-and-shadows) means a flash in the beam may be the entire observation.

## Fish in shallow water

Opaleye in very shallow pools should not be chased or cornered. The best views come when the observer stays still and lets reflections settle. That approach matches [the first scan](../fieldcraft/observation.md#the-first-scan), with the added discipline of watching shadows before bodies. Juveniles sometimes use warm, protected depressions in [sand and gravel](../habitats/sand-and-gravel.md#nursery-pools), where a few centimeters of water can be both nursery and hazard. Their movements reveal how porous the boundary is between tidepool, subtidal edge, and open kelp bed.

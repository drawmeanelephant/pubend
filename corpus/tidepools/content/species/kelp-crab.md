# Northern Kelp Crab

## Not quite a decorator

The northern kelp crab, *Pugettia producta*, is frequently described as a decorator crab, but many individuals carry little obvious decoration at all. Their smooth brown, red, or olive carapaces already echo kelp and rock, so camouflage may be structural as much as assembled. They eat algae and small animals, shelter beneath fronds, and move between submerged and intertidal spaces with ease. Their lives are tied closely to [the forest-rock boundary](../habitats/kelp-edges.md#where-forest-meets-rock), where food and cover continually rearrange.

Because they freeze when disturbed, kelp crabs are excellent subjects for practicing attention. A shape that first reads as a folded blade may become legs, eyes, and carapace after a few seconds. In this respect they complement the more mobile [opaleye](opaleye.md#algae-browser): both use the edge, but one relies on bursts of movement while the other often trusts stillness.

## Flexible shelter

Crabs found in surge channels may be using the habitat differently from those in open kelp beds. The protected surfaces of [the crawlspace forest](../habitats/surge-channels.md#the-crawlspace-forest) offer refuge from light and force, while detached kelp supplies portable cover. Their flexibility illustrates the tradeoffs in [hanging on](../life/adaptations.md#hanging-on) even though the crab is mobile rather than attached. At night, as noted in [the night journal](../journal/night-low-tide.md#lights-and-shadows), they may cross surprisingly open ground when shadows reduce the risk of exposure.

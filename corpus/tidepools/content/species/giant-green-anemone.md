# Giant Green Anemone

## Green in the flesh

The giant green anemone, *Anthopleura xanthogrammica*, owes much of its color to microscopic partners living in its tissues. Sunlight therefore matters to an animal that does not move toward it. In clear shallow pools the green can be vivid; in deeper or sediment-heavy water the same species may look duller. This combination of animal and photosynthetic partner makes it a memorable introduction to adaptation, but its colors should not be reduced to decoration. They sit inside the broader tradeoffs described under [water loss](../life/adaptations.md#water-loss).

When exposed, the anemone contracts, pulling tentacles inward and often covering the disk with bits of shell or gravel. The posture protects moisture and soft tissue, but it also hides the animal from casual observers. In shallow pans on [rocky benches](../habitats/rocky-benches.md#benches-as-stair-steps), the contrast between an expanded feeding anemone and a contracted exposed one can appear within a few vertical centimeters.

## Looking without touching

The mouth and tentacles are sensitive structures, not interactive exhibits. During the busy equinox tide described in [the spring journal](../journal/spring-equinox.md#when-the-crowd-arrives), many anemones remained contracted because shadows, footsteps, and probing fingers arrived constantly. The better method is the patient one in [look before touch](../fieldcraft/observation.md#look-before-touch): wait for water to cover the animal, then watch how prey, particles, and light move around it. In protected recesses of [surge channels](../habitats/surge-channels.md#the-crawlspace-forest), expanded individuals may persist where open rock has already dried.

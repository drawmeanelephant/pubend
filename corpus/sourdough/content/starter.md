# Build and Read the Starter

A starter is ready for bread when it rises predictably after feeding, smells yeasty and lightly acidic rather than sharp or neglected, and carries enough bubbles to look airy at its peak. For a beginner-friendly build, keep a small jar at room temperature and feed it twice daily with equal weights of flour and water, discarding enough before each feeding to keep the jar manageable. Whole-grain rye or whole-wheat flour usually speeds early activity because it carries more nutrients and microorganisms, while white flour gives a steadier texture once the culture is established.

The most useful signal is repeatability. If the jar doubles in four to six hours at roughly room temperature for several feedings in a row, it can probably leaven a loaf. A layer of gray liquid, an acetone-like smell, or a long lag after feeding usually means the culture is hungry, not dead. Before moving on to [flour, water, and salt](ingredients.md), feed the starter, let it reach a vigorous peak, and write down how long that took at your kitchen temperature.

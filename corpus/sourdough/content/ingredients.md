# Flour, Water, and Salt

For one moderate loaf, use 500 grams of bread flour, 350 grams of water, 100 grams of active starter, and 10 grams of fine salt. Bread flour provides enough protein for structure, but a substitution of 50 to 100 grams of whole-wheat flour adds flavor without making the dough unmanageably dense. Water that feels neutral on the skin is close enough for most kitchens; precise water temperature matters less than noticing whether the finished dough feels cool, warm, or in between.

Salt is not optional seasoning here. It tightens the dough, moderates fermentation, and keeps the crumb from tasting flat even when the loaf is otherwise well fermented. Measure it separately so it is not forgotten, and do not dissolve it in the water unless you have a reason. The quantities above are only a dependable baseline: flour absorbency, starter strength, and kitchen warmth will still require attention during mixing and fermentation.

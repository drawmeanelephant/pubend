# Proof the Shaped Loaf

Proofing is the final rise after shaping. At room temperature it may take only one to two hours, while a refrigerator proof of eight to sixteen hours slows fermentation, improves flavor, and makes the dough easier to score. The cold method is forgiving for a home schedule because it separates shaping from baking, but it is not magic: a dough that entered the basket exhausted will not recover strength in the refrigerator.

The traditional poke test can help, yet it is unreliable in very wet or very cold dough. A slowly partial spring-back suggests some remaining proofing capacity, while an immediate strong spring-back can mean the loaf is still tight. More useful is the total picture: how the dough behaved in bulk, how long it has rested, and whether the shaped loaf has relaxed and expanded slightly. When the loaf feels airy but not fragile, it is time for [the bake](baking.md).

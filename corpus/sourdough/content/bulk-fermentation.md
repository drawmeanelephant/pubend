# Bulk Fermentation

Bulk fermentation is the period when the yeast and bacteria multiply, produce gas, and build the flavor that distinguishes sourdough from a yeasted loaf. In a mild kitchen the first rise commonly lasts three to five hours, but the clock is only context. During the first half of the rise, fold the dough over itself every thirty to forty-five minutes two or three times. These folds organize the gluten, redistribute warmth, and give the baker a direct sense of how strength is developing.

## Reading the dough

Useful signs include a slightly domed surface, bubbles visible at the edges of the bowl, a dough that jiggles as a mass, and a texture that feels lighter than it did after mixing. If the dough still lies flat and dense, it probably needs more time or warmth; if it is slack, sticky, and tearing easily, it may have moved too far. Temperature differences of a few degrees can change the timeline by an hour or more, so record the room temperature instead of promising yourself a universal schedule.

End bulk fermentation while the dough still has enough strength to shape. Waiting for a dramatic doubling can be misleading because a wet dough spreads rather than climbing the bowl. A modest increase in volume paired with elasticity and visible aeration is a better target than a number by itself.

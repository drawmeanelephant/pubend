# Troubleshooting the First Loaves

A dense loaf most often points to weak leavening or under-fermentation, especially when the crumb is tight and the interior smells more floury than sour. A gummy center can also come from slicing before the loaf cooled, so confirm the simple causes first. An overly sour loaf is not necessarily fermented longer; excess acidity can come from a neglected starter, a warm and extended proof, or too much whole-grain flour for the schedule being used. Change one variable at a time and keep the batch size constant so comparisons mean something.

If the loaf spreads wide in the oven, look first at dough strength and proofing rather than adding more flour by habit. Weak shaping, exhausted dough, or a torn surface can all prevent oven spring. Revisit the [fermentation cues](bulk-fermentation.md#reading-the-dough) before blaming the scoring blade. The most useful corrective habit is a small record: starter peak time, dough temperature after mixing, bulk duration, proofing conditions, and a photograph of the crumb. After three or four loaves, patterns emerge that no universal recipe can predict.

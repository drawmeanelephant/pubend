# Mix the Dough

Combine the flour and water first and let them rest for twenty to thirty minutes before adding the starter and salt. This short autolyse hydrates the flour and begins gluten development without intensive kneading. Add the starter in small pieces, pinch the salt over the surface, then squeeze and fold the mixture until no dry pockets remain. The dough will look rough at first; the goal is uniform incorporation, not a smooth, machine-kneaded surface.

After mixing, cover the bowl and take the dough's temperature if you have a thermometer, or simply note whether it feels cool or slightly warm. A dough around 24°C usually moves at a convenient pace in a temperate kitchen. From this point onward, kneading is replaced by time and a few gentle folds, and the next decision is not mechanical but observational: [watching fermentation](bulk-fermentation.md#reading-the-dough) rather than obeying a fixed clock.

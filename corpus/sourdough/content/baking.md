# Bake in a Covered Pot

Heat a covered cast-iron pot in the oven at 230°C for at least half an hour. The heavy pot traps steam released by the dough, delaying crust formation so the loaf can expand before the exterior sets. Turn the cold or room-temperature loaf onto baking paper, score one decisive slash across the top with a sharp blade, and lower it carefully into the heated pot. A shallow, confident cut is more useful than several hesitant scratches.

Bake covered for twenty minutes, then remove the lid and continue baking until the crust is deeply browned, usually another twenty to twenty-five minutes. If the loaf sounds pale at the end, give it more time; color is flavor, and a firm crust also helps the crumb finish setting. Let the bread cool on a rack for at least two hours before slicing, even though the smell makes waiting difficult. Cutting too early releases steam and can leave the interior gummy.

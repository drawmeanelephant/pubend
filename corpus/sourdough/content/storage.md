# Store and Refresh the Bread

Once completely cool, keep the loaf cut-side down on a board or wrapped loosely in a clean cloth for the first day. Paper preserves the crust better than plastic, while a sealed plastic bag softens the crust but slows staling. Refrigeration is usually a poor compromise because it speeds starch retrogradation; freezing is better if the bread will not be eaten within two or three days. Slice the loaf before freezing so individual pieces can go directly into a toaster.

A dry slice can often be refreshed rather than discarded. Toast restores contrast, a brief warm-up in a moderate oven revives a larger piece, and very stale bread still works well in crumbs, croutons, or bread salad. If the loaf behaved unexpectedly, compare storage symptoms with mixing and fermentation notes before changing everything at once, then consult the final [troubleshooting notes](troubleshooting.md).

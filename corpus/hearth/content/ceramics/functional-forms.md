# Functional Forms

Functional ceramics succeed through use. A mug should balance when full, pour or drink without dribbling, and fit the hand without strain. A bowl should be stable, cleanable, and shaped for its intended food. A crock should allow weights to seat properly, leave enough headspace for brine, and open wide enough for cleaning. These requirements are more important than whether the form photographs well. The potter's test is not the moment of trimming but the hundredth ordinary use.

Design evaluation starts with measurements. Record capacity, wall thickness, foot diameter, lip shape, weight when empty, and weight in use. Then test the piece with water before trusting it with food. Does the handle heat up? Does the lid trap condensation? Can a sponge reach every interior corner? Food-use pieces also require confidence in clay maturity and glaze stability. Uncertain surfaces should never be rationalized as rustic. The broader safety framework appears in [glazes](glazes.md) and the firing evidence in [firing](firing.md).

The most important household crossover is fermentation. A crock intended for vegetables must work with the practices described in pantry [fermentation crocks](../pantry/fermentation/crocks.md): weights, water seals or covers, cleanability, and food-safe surfaces all matter. Storage jars for dry goods are less demanding but still need lids that seat honestly and interiors without deep crevices. Good functional pottery is collaborative even when made by one person, because the final design is shaped by the task it serves.

# Fiber Preparation

Fiber preparation is the quiet foundation of every textile project. Wool must be sorted, washed in water hot enough to loosen grease but not so agitated that it felts, and then dried with abundant airflow. Flax and other bast fibers follow a different path, but the principle is the same: remove what does not belong while preserving the length and strength of what does. A fleece that is carefully skirted at the beginning saves hours later, because second cuts, brittle tips, and vegetable matter never become evenly invisible in finished yarn.

Once clean and dry, fiber is opened with hand cards, combs, or a drum carder according to the yarn being planned. Carding makes a loftier preparation suited to woolen spinning; combing aligns longer fibers for a smoother worsted yarn. The choice is not a hierarchy of quality but a match between fiber, tool, and purpose. Spinners moving from preparation to the wheel can continue with [hand spinning](hand-spinning.md), and anyone planning color should read [natural dyeing](dyeing.md) before washing large batches, since some dye methods prefer fiber prepared without residual spinning oil.

Good preparation also depends on sound equipment. Carders, combs, drying racks, and baskets should be kept clean and dry; bent tines or splintered wooden parts damage fiber and make the work harder than it needs to be. Basic woodshop maintenance notes are useful when handles loosen or wooden drying frames need attention. Prepared fiber should be stored dry, loosely packed, and labeled with breed, source, and date so that later spinning decisions are based on evidence rather than memory.

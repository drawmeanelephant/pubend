# Alum Mordant

Alum, usually potassium aluminum sulfate, is the standard beginner mordant because it is predictable, widely available, and comparatively gentle on protein fibers. A common starting range is eight to twelve percent alum on the weight of dry fiber, dissolved in a small amount of hot water before being added to a larger bath. The fiber enters the bath clean, wet, and free of tangles. Heat is raised gradually and held below a hard boil so wool does not felt or lose its elasticity.

The bath can be used immediately or the fiber can cool and rest in it overnight. Resting often improves uptake without additional heat, which is useful when fuel or attention is limited. Afterward, some dyers rinse at once while others allow the fiber to cure for several days before dyeing. Either method can work; the important thing is consistency and a written record. For broader process context, return to the [mordants](../mordants.md) page or the main [natural dyeing](../../dyeing.md) notes.

Alum does not make every dye permanent. It improves the conditions for bonding, but the dye material itself still determines lightfastness and washfastness. This is why small test skeins are indispensable. Alum-mordanted wool often enters an [indigo vat](../indigo-vat.md) without a conventional dye-bath mordant step, so process notes should always identify which dye family is being used rather than assuming one sequence fits all color work.

# Sauerkraut

Sauerkraut is shredded cabbage preserved by lactic acid fermentation. The basic method is simple: remove damaged outer leaves, shred the cabbage evenly, weigh it, add salt at a measured percentage, massage or tamp until brine forms, and pack it tightly below the surface of that brine. The details are where quality lives. Uniform shreds ferment evenly. Enough salt controls texture and spoilage. A clean weight keeps cabbage submerged, while a loose cover allows gas to escape without inviting insects or dust.

During the first days the ferment should become pleasantly sour and aromatic. Small bubbles are normal. Kahm yeast can appear as a thin white film and can often be skimmed, but fuzzy mold, pink slime, or putrid odor means the batch should be discarded. Taste after the first week and then every few days, moving the crock cooler when the flavor reaches the balance the household enjoys. Fermentation never stops completely; it only slows. The broader principles are in [fermentation](../fermentation.md), and vessel guidance is in [fermentation crocks](crocks.md).

## Processing for the shelf

Fresh sauerkraut can be refrigerated for months, but shelf storage requires a tested heat-processing method. Heating changes texture and destroys the living culture, so it is a preservation decision rather than an improvement. If shelf-stable jars are needed, follow the acidity and processing guidance in a reliable canning manual and the general precautions in [canning](../canning.md). Never improvise processing times. Crocks and jars also matter here; hand-made forms discussed in the ceramics collection may be suitable for fermentation but not for heat processing unless their materials and thermal behavior are known.

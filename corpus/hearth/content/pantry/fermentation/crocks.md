# Fermentation Crocks

A fermentation crock is both container and microclimate. Its job is to hold vegetables under brine, protect them from airborne contamination, and tolerate salt and acid without leaching unwanted material into the food. Open crocks are simple and easy to clean but depend on careful weighting and covering. Water-sealed crocks use a rim filled with water to let gas escape while limiting incoming air. Both can work. The choice is less about tradition than about attention, space, and cleaning habits.

Before use, inspect a crock for cracks, crazing that traps residue, rough interior glaze, or chips around the rim. Wash it with hot water and a mild unscented detergent, rinse thoroughly, and dry it completely before storage. Weights should be food-safe and easy to remove with clean hands. Cloth covers should be washed between batches. If a batch fails, examine the equipment as carefully as the recipe. A narrow neck that prevents proper weighting or a lid that traps condensation can be the real problem.

Handmade ceramic vessels can be excellent fermentation tools when their clay and glaze are truly food-safe, fully vitrified, and shaped for cleaning. The ceramics collection discusses those requirements in [functional forms](../../ceramics/functional-forms.md). That cross-over is important: a beautiful crock is not automatically a safe one. When in doubt, use tested commercial food-grade equipment. The crock page belongs beside [sauerkraut](sauerkraut.md) because vessel choice and fermentation behavior cannot really be separated.

# Tool Setup

A small shop works when every tool has a place, a purpose, and a ready edge. The foundation is a stable bench with a way to hold work: a vise, bench dogs, holdfasts, or simple clamps. Measuring and marking tools come next, because a sharp plane cannot correct a badly laid-out joint. A practical starter kit includes a square, marking knife, tape or rule, chisels, a plane, a saw suited to the intended work, and a means of sharpening all of them. Buy fewer tools than enthusiasm suggests, then learn them thoroughly.

Setup is not a one-time arrangement. Tools migrate during a project, rust during damp weather, and become dangerous when their handles or fences loosen. A weekly reset takes less time than searching for a missing marking knife in the middle of a glue-up. Keep the bench surface flat enough for honest assembly, protect it with sacrificial boards when chiseling, and store measuring tools where they cannot be knocked onto concrete. The habit of maintenance connects directly to [sharpening](sharpening.md), since an edge tool stored badly often needs corrective grinding rather than routine honing.

The shop's layout should reflect the order of operations in [joinery](joinery.md): rough stock preparation, accurate layout, cutting, fitting, assembly, and finishing. Finishing supplies belong in a clean, ventilated area rather than beside sawdust piles; see [finishes](finishes.md) before mixing the two. A well-kept shop also supports craft tools beyond furniture, including the combs and drying frames used in textile preparation.

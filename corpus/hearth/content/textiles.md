# Textiles

The textile collection gathers the household fiber arts into one working shelf: preparing fleece and flax, spinning yarn, coloring it with plant or vat dyes, and keeping finished cloth in service through repair. These crafts share materials, but each has its own rhythm. Fiber preparation is slow and tactile, spinning rewards small daily practice, dyeing requires careful notes, and mending asks for close attention to the cloth that already exists. Start with [fiber preparation](textiles/fiber-preparation.md) if the raw material is unfamiliar, or move directly to [hand spinning](textiles/hand-spinning.md) when clean fiber is ready.

The pages in this section treat textile work as a practical household system rather than a display craft. Equipment is modest, measurements are written down, and the emphasis is on repeatable results. The [natural dyeing](textiles/dyeing.md) notes explain why clean fiber and disciplined record keeping matter before color ever enters the pot, while [mending](textiles/mending.md) closes the loop by extending the life of woven and knitted goods. Because drying, moisture, and wooden tools cross craft boundaries, a few pages point toward the pantry and woodshop collections where those subjects are treated in their own terms.

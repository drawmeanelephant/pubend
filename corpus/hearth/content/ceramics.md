# Ceramics

The ceramics collection focuses on useful household vessels rather than decorative sculpture. Clay preparation, glaze discipline, firing, and functional design form one continuous chain. A beautiful bowl that cannot be cleaned, a crock whose glaze is uncertain, or a mug that cracks in daily use is not successful merely because it emerged from the kiln. Utility ceramics ask for humility: test tiles, measured batches, careful firing notes, and a willingness to reject pieces that do not meet food-use standards.

Start with [clay preparation](ceramics/clay-preparation.md), because inconsistent clay creates problems that no amount of careful glazing can repair. The [glazes](ceramics/glazes.md) section treats glaze as chemistry and process control, not as paint. [Firing](ceramics/firing.md) connects the earlier choices to heatwork, while [functional forms](ceramics/functional-forms.md) evaluates whether finished shapes actually serve the household. The strongest cross-connection is with the pantry: fermentation depends on vessels that are safe, cleanable, and appropriately shaped.

Ceramics has a longer feedback loop than many crafts. A mistake in wedging may appear only after firing; a glaze imbalance may look fine until the vessel is filled with hot liquid. Records therefore matter at every stage. Label clay bodies, glaze batches, kiln shelves, and test tiles. Photographs help, but written measurements are more durable. The collection favors a few reliable forms repeated well over a shelf of accidental experiments.

# Paprika

Paprika is ground dried red pepper, but the label covers a broad range of flavors depending on the peppers and processing. Sweet paprika is mild, round, and brightly colored; hot paprika carries more capsaicin; smoked paprika is dried over smoke and can make a dish taste grilled even when no fire is involved. Hungarian and Spanish styles are not interchangeable in every recipe, but all forms share an emphasis on pepper flesh rather than seeds or resinous spice heat.

Paprika is sensitive to heat. Scorching it in a dry pan can create bitterness very quickly, so it is often added after onions have softened or bloomed briefly in warm fat off the direct hottest heat. Its gentle body supports [chili](chili.md) when a dish needs redness and pepper flavor without overpowering burn. Paprika also sits naturally beside sumac, since paprika supplies sweet depth while sumac contributes acid-like brightness. In spice rubs, a little cumin keeps paprika's sweetness from becoming flat.

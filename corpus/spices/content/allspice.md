# Allspice

Allspice is the dried unripe berry of *Pimenta dioica*, not a mixture of other spices. It earned its English name because its aroma seems to recall cinnamon, clove, and nutmeg at once, with an additional peppery edge. That complexity makes it unusually efficient: a few berries can make a braise, pickle, cake, or meat seasoning taste layered without requiring a long list of ingredients. Jamaican cooking uses it prominently, especially in jerk seasoning and stews.

Whole allspice berries are useful in infusions because they can be removed before their woody intensity becomes too strong. Ground allspice is convenient but potent, and it should be added in small increments. The spice naturally overlaps with [cloves](cloves.md), [cinnamon](cinnamon.md), and nutmeg, so blends containing several of them need restraint. When used well, allspice does not imitate those spices exactly; it creates a darker, more unified warmth that is particularly valuable in savory food.

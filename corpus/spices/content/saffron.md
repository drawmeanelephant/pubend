# Saffron

Saffron consists of the dried stigmas of *Crocus sativus*, harvested by hand and sold in tiny quantities. Its flavor is difficult to imitate: floral, honeyed, metallic, hay-like, and slightly bitter, with a color that moves from gold to deep orange depending on concentration. Because it is expensive, adulteration is common, and whole threads are safer than suspiciously cheap powder. Good saffron should smell potent even before it touches liquid.

## Infusing threads

Saffron releases more flavor and color when crushed lightly and steeped in warm water, milk, or stock before use. Adding dry threads directly to a large pot often gives uneven results. The spice is central to dishes such as paella, tahdig, bouillabaisse, and saffron rice, where it should remain perceptible rather than buried under stronger seasonings. [Cardamom](cardamom.md) can heighten its floral side in desserts, while a small amount of turmeric sometimes supports color in cheaper blends but cannot reproduce saffron's aroma. In milk sweets it meets [vanilla](vanilla.md#using-the-seeds) gracefully because both ingredients reward gentle heat.

# Black Pepper

Black peppercorns are the dried, unripe berries of *Piper nigrum*, and their heat is only part of their value. Freshly cracked pepper is resinous, citrusy, woody, and slightly floral before the sharp piperine burn arrives. Those high notes disappear quickly from pre-ground pepper, which is why a mill or mortar matters more here than for almost any other everyday spice. Black pepper is a finishing spice and a foundational seasoning at the same time: it can sit quietly behind salt in a soup or dominate a simple dish such as cacio e pepe.

In cooked spice mixtures pepper behaves differently from seed spices such as [cumin](cumin.md) and coriander. Long simmering mutes its brighter aromatics but leaves a steady warmth, so pepper added early seasons the body of a dish while pepper added near the end defines the surface. White pepper comes from ripe peppercorns with the outer skin removed and has a funkier, more fermented edge; green peppercorns are fresher and softer but usually sold brined or freeze-dried. For all forms, the shared [storage guidance](index.md#buying-and-storage) applies especially strongly because ground pepper can become flat within weeks.

# Turmeric

Turmeric is a rhizome in the ginger family, most often sold dried and ground into a deep yellow powder. Its flavor is earthy, bitter, musky, and mildly peppery, while its color is immediate and persistent. Turmeric is sometimes treated as if it were only a coloring agent, but a dish that relies on it solely for yellow color misses its real role: it gives lentils, rice, pickles, and vegetable dishes a dry, grounding bass note. Too much, however, turns chalky and bitter.

Turmeric benefits from fat and from companions that sharpen or brighten it. [Black pepper](black-pepper.md) is the classic partner, not only because of popular claims about absorption but because pepper cuts turmeric's flat earthiness. Ginger supplies heat and aromatic lift, while cumin reinforces the darker savory side. Because ground turmeric is the common form, freshness still matters: stale turmeric loses aroma first and leaves behind mostly pigment and bitterness.

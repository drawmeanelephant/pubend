# Sumac

Culinary sumac is made from the dried red drupes of certain *Rhus* species, ground into a coarse, tangy, burgundy-colored powder. Its dominant impression is sourness, but it is not simply powdered citrus: sumac is fruity, slightly astringent, earthy, and salty-adjacent in the way it makes food taste more complete. It is especially useful when a dish needs brightness without additional liquid, making it valuable in salads, grilled meats, rice, onions, and dips.

Sumac should usually be added late or at the table because prolonged heat dulls its fresh acidity. It pairs naturally with [paprika](paprika.md), since paprika gives sweet red-pepper depth and sumac cuts across it. Coriander reinforces sumac's lemony side without making the mixture taste thin, while bay laurel can provide a savory base in marinades and braised dishes where sumac is added near the end. Good sumac tastes lively and tart; stale sumac turns mostly brown and dusty.

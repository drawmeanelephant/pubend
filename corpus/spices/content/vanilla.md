# Vanilla

Vanilla is the cured fruit pod of an orchid, most often *Vanilla planifolia*, and its aroma is the result of a long fermentation and drying process. Real vanilla is sweet, creamy, woody, floral, and faintly smoky, with hundreds of aroma compounds rather than a single simple flavor. Whole pods, paste, and extract each have different uses. Extract is convenient and consistent; paste gives visible seeds and strong flavor; whole pods offer control when infusing milk, cream, sugar, or syrup.

## Using the seeds

To use a pod, split it lengthwise, scrape the seeds into the liquid or mixture, and add the empty pod as well while infusing. The pod still carries aroma and can be rinsed, dried, and buried in sugar afterward. Vanilla softens the sharper edges of [cardamom](cardamom.md), deepens cinnamon, and gives nutmeg a creamier frame in custards. It can also complement [saffron](saffron.md) in rice puddings and milk desserts, but both ingredients are aromatic enough that the quantities should stay small.

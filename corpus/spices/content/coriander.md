# Coriander

Coriander seed is the dried fruit of the cilantro plant, but its flavor is much gentler and sweeter than the polarizing fresh leaf. Good coriander smells of lemon peel, warm nuts, and a faint floral resin. Because it is aromatic without being aggressive, it is one of the most useful structure-building spices: it thickens the flavor profile of a blend, rounds sharp edges, and gives acidity somewhere to sit. Whole seeds are light and easy to crush, and even pre-ground coriander is less fragile than many ground spices, though it still fades with time.

Coriander is most often discussed beside cumin, and the pairing is real: cumin supplies depth while coriander supplies lift. It can also borrow the same blooming method described under [cumin](cumin.md#toasting-and-blooming), though coriander tolerates slightly longer toasting because it is less bitter. In lighter dishes it meets [cardamom](cardamom.md) and fennel naturally, especially where citrus, dairy, or green herbs need a spice bridge rather than a dominant seasoning.

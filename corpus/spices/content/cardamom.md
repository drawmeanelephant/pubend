# Cardamom

Green cardamom is intensely aromatic, combining eucalyptus-like coolness, lemon, resin, and a sweetness that can seem almost perfumed. The small pods protect black seeds whose oils are far more expressive than the pale hull, so buying whole pods and cracking them as needed gives the best flavor. Cardamom is used in rice dishes, curries, coffee, pastries, and milk-based desserts because it reads as both savory and sweet. Black cardamom is a different ingredient entirely: larger, smoky, camphorous, and better suited to long-cooked savory food.

Because cardamom is volatile and expensive, the practical advice echoes the atlas-wide storage guidance: keep pods sealed, grind only the seeds, and avoid large pre-ground jars. It pairs naturally with [cinnamon](cinnamon.md) and [vanilla](vanilla.md) in sweet dishes, while a small amount can lift coriander-based savory blends. Too much quickly turns medicinal, so cardamom is best treated as a precise top note rather than a background bulk spice.

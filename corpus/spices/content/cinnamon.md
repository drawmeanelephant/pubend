# Cinnamon

Cinnamon is the dried inner bark of several *Cinnamomum* species, and the difference between cassia and Ceylon cinnamon matters in both flavor and use. Cassia, the common supermarket form in many countries, is thick, strong, sweet-hot, and slightly bitter. Ceylon cinnamon is thinner, more fragile, and more floral, with a delicate citrus quality. Both are useful, but cassia tolerates robust baking and long simmering while Ceylon cinnamon is better where its complexity will not be buried.

Cinnamon crosses the sweet-savory boundary more easily than its dessert reputation suggests. It gives depth to meat braises, rice pilafs, lentils, tomato sauces, and squash dishes, especially when balanced by cloves or freshened by [cardamom](cardamom.md). The spice is powerful enough to flatten a blend if used casually, so small quantities and tasting matter. For drinks and milk desserts, the controlled infusion approach used for [cloves](cloves.md#controlled-infusions) is also a good model: steep, taste, and remove the bark before its tannic edge overtakes the aroma.

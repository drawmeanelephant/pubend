# Ginger

Dried ginger and fresh ginger are related but not interchangeable. Fresh ginger is juicy, pungent, citrusy, and sharp; dried ginger is warmer, more woody, and more concentrated in a baking-spice direction. Ground ginger belongs in cakes, biscuits, spice blends, and some savory rubs, while fresh ginger behaves more like an aromatic vegetable. This entry concerns the dried spice, though the comparison is useful because substituting one for the other changes both moisture and flavor.

Ground ginger pairs naturally with [turmeric](turmeric.md) in golden spice mixtures and with [chili](chili.md) in marinades where heat needs shape rather than simple escalation. It can also bridge sweet spices such as cardamom and cinnamon because it is warming without being sweet itself. As with other ground spices, buy small quantities and smell the jar: fresh ground ginger should be bright and peppery, not merely dusty and hot.

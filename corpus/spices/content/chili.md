# Chili

Dried chili is not one spice but a family of ingredients made from different capsicum varieties, each with its own balance of heat, fruit, bitterness, smoke, and color. Flaked chili gives quick, visible heat; whole dried chilies can be toasted and rehydrated for sauces; fine chili powders vary enormously in strength. Good chili seasoning should taste like pepper fruit first and capsaicin second. If a blend offers only pain, it is usually hiding stale or poorly chosen chilies.

Chili works best when the surrounding spices explain its heat. [Cumin](cumin.md) gives it an earthy base, [paprika](paprika.md) can add color and sweetness without much additional burn, and black pepper contributes a sharper, shorter-lived heat that sits above chili's slower glow. Because capsaicin is stable, old chili powder can remain hot even after its aroma fades. Freshness therefore shows up less as diminished heat than as diminished fruit and complexity.

# Cumin

Cumin seed is small, ridged, and assertive, with a flavor that moves between earthy, nutty, bitter, and faintly smoky. It is indispensable in many cooking traditions precisely because it can make a dish taste grounded without making it taste heavy. Whole seeds have a rugged, toasted-grain character; ground cumin is more immediate but loses intensity quickly. Its bitterness is useful in moderation, especially beside the brighter citrus notes of [coriander](coriander.md) or the direct heat of [chili](chili.md).

## Toasting and blooming

Cumin rewards careful heat. A minute or two in a dry pan turns whole seeds fragrant and crisp, but the line between toasted and scorched is short, so they should leave the pan as soon as their aroma deepens. Ground cumin is better bloomed briefly in oil with onions, garlic, or tomato than dry-toasted on its own. That technique also suits dishes where black pepper is added late: cumin establishes the warm base while pepper keeps its sharper top notes for the finish.

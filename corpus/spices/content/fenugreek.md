# Fenugreek

Fenugreek seeds are hard, angular, and strongly aromatic, with a curious combination of bitterness, roasted nuttiness, and a maple-like sweetness that becomes more obvious after cooking. They are essential in many South Asian and Ethiopian flavor profiles and appear in spice blends, pickles, and sauces. Raw seeds can be harsh, so they usually benefit from toasting, soaking, or frying in oil. Fenugreek leaves, often sold dried as kasuri methi, are a related but distinct ingredient with a greener aroma.

Fenugreek rewards careful balancing. Cumin reinforces its earthy roasted qualities, while fennel softens the bitterness with sweetness. [Mustard seed](mustard-seed.md) shares fenugreek's affinity for hot oil and pickling brines, though mustard is sharper and more volatile. Because fenugreek's maple note can dominate a dish, it is best measured conservatively until the cook knows how a particular batch behaves.

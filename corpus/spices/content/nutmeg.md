# Nutmeg

Nutmeg is the seed of *Myristica fragrans*, usually sold whole because grating it fresh preserves its warm, woody, slightly sweet aroma. The same fruit also produces mace, the lacy outer covering of the seed, which is more delicate and more expensive. Nutmeg is at home in cream sauces, custards, mashed vegetables, sausages, cakes, and eggy drinks. It rarely needs to dominate; its best effect is often a rounding sensation that makes dairy taste richer and cooked vegetables taste more finished.

Fresh nutmeg is one of the clearest arguments for whole spices. A whole seed keeps well, while ground nutmeg becomes dull and slightly dusty as its oils dissipate. It combines easily with [cinnamon](cinnamon.md) and [vanilla](vanilla.md) in desserts, but it also deserves a place in savory cooking beside black pepper, bay, and browned butter. In small quantities it can even echo the sweet warmth of cloves without cloves' numbing force.

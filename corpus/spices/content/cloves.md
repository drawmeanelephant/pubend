# Cloves

Cloves are the unopened flower buds of an evergreen tree, dried until they resemble small nails. Their flavor is concentrated, numbing, sweet, woody, and almost medicinal, driven strongly by eugenol. Few spices punish overuse as quickly. One or two cloves can perfume a pot of rice or a batch of poaching liquid; several too many can make the entire dish taste like a throat lozenge. Whole cloves are preferable because they can be counted, controlled, and removed.

## Controlled infusions

Cloves work best when their extraction is deliberate. In mulled wine, chai, broth, or syrup, add them near the beginning only if the liquid will be strained promptly, or add them later for a shorter steep. They have natural affinities with [cinnamon](cinnamon.md), star anise, and allspice, but those blends work because each spice contributes a different part of the same warm spectrum. Ground cloves should be measured with particular care, since their intensity disperses invisibly through a mixture.
